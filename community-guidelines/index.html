Kinno community

Community guidelines.

What Kinno is for

Kinno exists so adults can make real friends in person. Everything here comes down to one idea: turn up, be kind, and look after each other.

Most people get this right without thinking about it. These guidelines are for the moments where it helps to know what's expected, and so everyone knows what we'll act on.

Where these apply. Everywhere Kinno takes you: the Pod or event itself, the drinks afterwards, the walk to the station, Pod and private chats in the app, and unwanted contact that carries on somewhere else once you've swapped details.

Turning up

A Pod is a handful of people who've set aside time to meet you. Not turning up is the one thing that affects everyone else.

  • Only join a Pod you intend to attend.
  • If plans change, cancel in the app as early as you can, so we can try to fill your place.
  • Running late? Say so in the Pod chat. Nobody minds ten minutes. They mind being left guessing.
  • Life happens. One cancellation isn't a problem. A pattern of no-shows is.

We never charge fines or fees for missing a Pod. Repeated no-shows lower your reliability standing and tier, which means you're offered Pods less often. An absence only counts against you if at least two Pod members say you weren't there, and most of the people who answered agree.

At the Pod

  • Bring everyone in. If someone's gone quiet, ask them something. The quietest person at the table is often the one who most wanted to come.
  • Share the airtime. Notice if you've been talking for a while.
  • Come as yourself. No one expects you to be the most interesting person in the room.
  • Sort the bill fairly. Pay for what you ordered, and don't leave someone covering you.
  • Come to the Pod, not just the group chat. Phones away where you can.
  • Don't bring anyone who isn't in your Pod, even another Kinno member. The group was matched for the people in it.

If the conversation turns to politics, religion or anything heated, that's fine, as long as everyone stays respectful and nobody's pressed to take a side. Sharing your views is fine. Campaigning, recruiting or fundraising isn't.

Respect

These are the things we act on quickly.

  • No harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking or abuse, in person or in chat.
  • No discrimination based on race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, disability, sex, gender identity, sexuality, age, pregnancy, marital status or background.
  • No unwanted sexual attention. Kinno is for friendship. If someone hasn't welcomed it, don't go there.
  • Take no for an answer, whether it's about a drink, a second Pod, a phone number or a hug.
  • No pressure to drink. Plenty of people don't, and nobody should have to explain why.
  • Don't turn up drunk or high.
  • Don't sell anything, recruit for a business or scheme, or use a Pod to campaign.
  • Never ask another member for money, or for financial details.
  • No pick-up or seduction tactics, wingman games, or treating a Pod as practice.
  • No scams, fake investments, counterfeit goods, drugs, weapons or anything illegal.
  • No shocking, graphic or violent content, and nothing that encourages self-harm.
  • No offering medical, legal or other regulated services. We don't check anyone's credentials.

What this looks like in practice. Someone spends the evening pitching an investment opportunity to the table. Someone keeps steering the conversation to a Pod member's appearance after they've changed the subject twice. Someone turns up having had a few too many and gets loud with the venue staff. All three are worth reporting.

Staying safe

Kinno doesn't run background checks on members, and we're not at your Pod or event. Nobody in a Pod is in charge either — you're all there as equals. A few habits worth keeping:

  • Stay at the venue. If the group moves on somewhere private, it's fine to call it a night.
  • Sort your own way home.
  • Tell someone where you're going, and add emergency contacts in the app so you can reach them from the "Feeling unsafe?" screen. Ask them first.
  • Keep your details to yourself until you're comfortable. There's no need to share your address, workplace or finances with people you've just met.
  • Leave whenever you want. You never owe anyone an explanation.
  • Trust your gut. If something feels off, it's enough of a reason to go.
  • Keep your drink and your belongings in sight.
  • Pace yourself if you're drinking, and stick to the legal drinking age where you are.

If you feel unsafe, call your local emergency number — 000 in Australia, 999 or 112 in the UK. The "Feeling unsafe?" screen in the app opens your phone's Messages app with a ready-made message for you to send, but it isn't a substitute for emergency services, and we can't promise anyone will respond. Tell us afterwards by reporting it, so we can act.

Be who you say you are

  • Use your real name and recent photos of yourself. A clear photo of your face, without heavy filters, text or memes. People should recognise you when you walk in.
  • One account per person.
  • Don't pretend to be someone else, or make up details to get matched differently.
  • Rate honestly. Confirm who actually turned up, and base ratings on what happened, not on whether you clicked with someone. A Vibe Check isn't a review: it affects someone's reliability standing on Kinno. Using it to punish someone, or marking them down because of who they are, is a serious breach. At the moment, not confirming someone was there counts the same as saying they weren't, so please answer for everyone in your Pod. Other members never see your answers or who gave them, though our safety team can.
  • Report in good faith. Reports are for behaviour that worries you, not for getting back at someone. Making something up, or reporting the same person over and over without cause, can affect your own standing on Kinno.
  • Don't try to game the system: no inflating your own ratings, swapping confirmations, or getting around limits on your account.

Other people's privacy

What people share in a Pod belongs to that Pod.

  • Ask before taking photos, and before posting them anywhere.
  • Don't screenshot or share chats outside Kinno without everyone's agreement.
  • Don't pass on someone's details, including their full name, phone number, workplace or where they live.
  • Don't look people up and turn up where they'll be.
  • Don't add people to mailing lists or message them about anything they didn't ask for.
  • Keep it in the app until you both want to swap other contact details.

If you host an event

Hosting comes with a bit more responsibility, because you'll see your attendees' names, photos, tier and safety ratings.

  • Use attendee details only to run your event. Don't share them, save them, or contact people afterwards about anything else.
  • Describe your event accurately, including the address, cost and what to expect.
  • Only list events you have the right to run, at a venue that's expecting you.
  • Make people feel welcome, especially anyone arriving alone.
  • Deal with problems or tell us. If someone's behaviour is a concern, report it.

Hosts who misuse attendee information lose hosting access, and may have their account closed.

Reporting, blocking and what happens next

Blocking. You can block anyone. They won't see your profile, though it stays visible to them until any Pod you already share has happened. You'll still see theirs, so you can unblock or report them. We won't put you in the same Pod again. Since we don't match you into events the way we do Pods, you could both end up at the same one, though only the host sees who's coming.

Reporting. Report anyone whose behaviour concerns you, from a rude comment to something that made you uncomfortable. Reports go to our team, not to the person you reported. If you report a chat, a copy of up to the last 50 messages goes with it so our team can see what happened — in a Pod chat that includes everyone's messages, not just the person you reported. We'll never tell them who reported them. If you're in danger, call your local emergency number first.

What we do. We look at every report. Depending on what we find, we might have a quiet word, lower someone's reliability standing, offer them Pods less often, suspend them, or remove them from Kinno. If a report is serious, we may freeze an account straight away while we look into it. We may report serious incidents to the police, and we always report anything involving a child.

Zero tolerance. Anything that exploits or endangers a child means immediate removal and a report to the authorities. The same goes for violence, threats and sexual misconduct.

How we decide. A person reviews every report, not an automated system. We look at what was reported, anything the people involved tell us, and whether something similar has happened before.

If a report is about you. Where we can, we'll tell you by email which rule we think was broken, and why in general terms, and give you a chance to reply before we decide. For a ban we often can't explain, because doing so can put the person who reported you at risk, and sometimes we have to act first for someone's safety. We'll never tell you who reported you.

If you think we got it wrong. Email safety@kinnoapp.com if it is about a suspension or removal, or support@kinnoapp.com for anything else. You have 30 days, and a person will look at it again. If you're still unhappy, in the UK you can take a complaint to Ofcom, and in Australia to the eSafety Commissioner.

Behaviour outside Kinno. If something happens on another app or by text after you've swapped details, we can't moderate that platform. Tell us anyway: if someone you met through Kinno is behaving badly elsewhere, that affects whether they belong here. We'll ask you for something we can actually look at — screenshots, or a clear account of what happened and when — and we'll give the other person a chance to respond before we act.

If we remove someone. They can't come back under a new account, and please don't post or arrange things on their behalf.

You can always walk away. Leaving a Pod early, blocking someone, or taking a break from Kinno are all fine. Pulling out of a confirmed Pod at the last minute is different: that's recorded as a no-show, so cancel in the app as early as you can. Leaving a Pod because you've blocked someone in it doesn't count against you, once in any 90-day period.
